From HL7 to FHIR: Translating Legacy Experience into Future-Proof Skills

By Sherminta J. Lawrence

🔄 This post is part of an ongoing series on translating HL7 experience into FHIR-first roles. New posts drop weekly(ish)!

Why This Post?

Working with HL7 v2 gave me deep roots in healthcare data interoperability. But as the industry shifts to FHIR, I realized my experience wasn’t obsolete per se, it was foundational. This post is for other interface analysts making that same transition.

My Most Recent Experience: HL7-Centric Integration, with a Dash of Data Analysis

I spent the last three years in a lab-focused, ancillary interface environment, configuring HL7 v2 interfaces between LIS and EMR systems. I worked with tools like Mirth and Cerner’s middleware, using VB and JavaScript to manipulate segments and route messages through various hospital workflows. The work was essential but very niche. Without room to advance outside of management, I realized my career growth required learning new tools and expanding into others like FHIR, SQL, and Python. I have experience with FHIR. Python was actually one of the first languages I learned, and SQL became the next mountain to climb. I mention all of this because I want to show how I made the leap from HL7 to FHIR—and how you can too. In this series, I’ll walk through my transition, the skills I leaned into, and how I visualized the pivot in my portfolio and practice.

What Transfers and What Doesn’t

HL7 and FHIR share the same end goal: seamless healthcare communication. However, they differ in approach.

  • What’s familiar: Mapping, debugging, troubleshooting workflows
  • What’s different: APIs, JSON, RESTful logic, and a resource-based mindset

Programming Skills That Bridge the Gap

To make the leap, I focused on Python, JSON, and REST fundamentals. I started using Postman, learned how to read FHIR resource definitions, and tested endpoints manually before writing transformations. I built middleware that acted as translators between the two systems, applying the same logic I’d used for HL7 mapping, just with modern tools.

Career Impact: From Interface Analyst to FHIR Expert

Making the leap from HL7-focused integration work to FHIR-based development doesn't mean starting over—it means evolving. The fundamentals of interfacing, mapping, and clinical workflows are still core. What changes is the stack, the tools, and the strategic value you bring.

This shift from HL7 to FHIR isn’t about abandoning the old — it’s about making your experience portable and future-ready. Don’t discard your HL7 skills. Use them to build the next generation of healthcare infrastructure.
